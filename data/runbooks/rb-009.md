## Symptoms

- The order is in `paid` and has not been packed within 24 hours, with no payment hold on the timeline.
- The warehouse has noted a stock shortage, or the item shows zero stock in the warehouse assigned to the order.
- Less often, the order reached `packed` but the parcel cannot be found when the carrier arrives.
- The customer asks why a paid order has not moved.

## Checks

1. Rule out a payment hold first. A `payment_hold` event without a later `payment_released` is rb-004, not a stock problem.
2. Ask the dispatch desk to confirm the shortage by a physical count. System stock is sometimes wrong in both directions.
3. Check stock for the same item in the other warehouse in the region: WH-BOM and WH-PNQ in the west, WH-BLR and WH-MAA in the south.
4. Ask the dispatch desk for the restock date. A date from the supplier is firm; "expected soon" is not a date.

## Resolution

1. If the other warehouse in the region has stock, ask the dispatch desk to transfer the order there. This adds about one day. Tell the customer.
2. If there is a firm restock date within five days, offer the customer the choice of waiting or cancelling.
3. If there is no stock and no firm date, offer three options: wait without a date, accept a substitute of equal or higher value at no extra charge, or cancel.
4. Record the customer's choice in a ticket note in their own words.
5. If the customer chooses to cancel, cancel the order under rb-028. The reason is `out_of_stock`.
6. If the customer does not reply within two days, send one reminder. After two more days, cancel the order under rb-028 rather than hold the customer's money.

## Compensation

- A cancelled order that was paid for is refunded in full under the reason `cancelled`. See rb-022.
- If the customer chooses to wait, the time in `paid` counts as delay, and a goodwill refund is possible once the threshold for their tier is reached. See rb-021.
- A substitute of higher value is sent at the original price. The difference is not recorded as a refund.

## Escalation

- Escalate to L2 for any refund. L1 engineers record the recommendation.
- Escalate to a supervisor when a substitute costs more than INR 2,000 above the original item.
- If ten or more orders for the same item are affected, tell a supervisor so the item can be removed from sale.
