## Symptoms

- The ticket belongs to a premium customer and has been open for more than 48 hours.
- The underlying problem can be anything: an order stuck in packed, a carrier exception, a payment hold.
- The ticket may have been handled correctly so far. Age alone triggers this runbook.
- The customer may have written more than once, or asked for a manager.

## Checks

1. Confirm the customer's tier on the customer record. Do not rely on the customer's own description.
2. Confirm the ticket's age from its creation time, not from the last note.
3. Read all the ticket notes, so the customer is not asked anything twice.
4. Identify the cause from the order timeline and find the runbook for it. This runbook changes who handles the ticket and how fast. It does not replace the fix.
5. Check the ticket's priority. A premium customer's ticket should be one level above the normal level for the problem.

## Resolution

1. Correct the priority if it was not raised when the ticket was opened.
2. Escalate the ticket to L2 with a note that states the cause, what has been done and what you recommend next. Include a refund recommendation only if the order qualifies.
3. The L2 engineer who takes the ticket owns it until it is resolved, and tells the customer so by name.
4. The owner contacts the customer at least once a day with a real update, even when the update is that the carrier has not yet replied.
5. Consider an expedited re-shipment under rb-024. For a premium customer it is available as soon as the order is stuck and stock exists.
6. Write to the customer in the way the `premium-customer-handling` document, section `tone`, describes: specific, brief, and with no promise that has not been approved.

## Compensation

- Premium status lowers the goodwill threshold to 72 hours and raises the cap to 15%. It changes nothing else. See rb-021.
- The standard offer remains 10% of the order value.
- A full refund needs the same evidence as for any customer. See rb-022.
- Priority handling is not compensation and is never offered in place of a refund that is due.

## Escalation

- L1 to L2: every premium ticket older than 48 hours, whether or not a refund is involved.
- L2 to supervisor: a premium ticket older than seven days, or a premium customer who asks to close their account.
- If the L2 owner is away for more than a day, they hand the ticket to a named colleague and tell the customer.
