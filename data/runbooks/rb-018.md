## Symptoms

- The order is `delivered`, and the order timeline ends with a `delivered` event.
- The customer says nothing arrived, that nobody came to the address, or that they cannot find the parcel.
- The `delivered` event records who received the parcel: the customer, a family member, the security desk or a neighbour.
- Reports usually come within a day or two of the delivery scan.

## Checks

1. Read the `delivered` event and note the time and who received the parcel. In most cases the parcel was accepted by someone else.
2. Ask the customer to check with family, neighbours, the building's security desk or reception, and any safe place they have used before.
3. Confirm the delivery address on the order with the customer.
4. Check when the delivery was marked. Some riders scan parcels as delivered at the start of a route; a parcel marked delivered in the early morning often arrives later the same day.
5. Check whether the customer has reported non-receipt on earlier orders.

## Resolution

1. If the receiver was not the customer, tell the customer who is recorded as having received the parcel and ask them to check with that person.
2. If the parcel is still missing after 24 hours, request proof of delivery from the carrier: the delivery photo, the signature or name, and the rider's location at the time of the scan.
3. If the proof matches the customer's address, share the photo with the customer. This resolves most cases.
4. If the proof shows a different address or no proof exists, ask the carrier to open an investigation. Carriers decide within seven days.
5. If the carrier recovers the parcel, arrange redelivery. If the carrier confirms it cannot be found, a `lost_in_transit_confirmed` event is recorded and the case continues under rb-013.

## Compensation

- No refund is possible while the order is recorded as delivered and the carrier's investigation is open. Say this plainly, and say what happens next.
- If the carrier confirms the loss, the customer may choose a full refund or a re-shipment. See rb-013 and rb-022.
- A goodwill refund for delay is not available on a delivered order.
- For a premium customer, an L2 engineer may offer an expedited re-shipment under rb-024 while the investigation is open, when the proof of delivery is clearly wrong.

## Escalation

- Escalate to L2 when the proof of delivery does not match the customer's address, or when the carrier has not replied within 48 hours.
- Escalate to a supervisor when a customer has reported non-receipt on three or more orders in six months.
