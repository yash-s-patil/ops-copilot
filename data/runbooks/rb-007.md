## Symptoms

- The customer was told a refund was approved and says the money has not arrived.
- The ticket or the order shows a refund with one of four statuses: approved, sent to payments, paid or failed.
- Sometimes no refund exists at all: the customer was told one was "being looked at", or assumes a cancelled order refunds itself instantly.

## Checks

1. Open the refund on the order and read its status. Do this before saying anything to the customer about dates.
2. If no refund is recorded, check the ticket notes for a refund request that is still waiting for approval, or one that was rejected or expired. A request waiting for a supervisor is not a refund yet.
3. For a refund that is paid, note the payment date and the provider reference.
4. For a refund that failed, read the failure reason. The common ones are a closed card and a bank that rejected the credit.

## Resolution

By status:

1. **Approved or sent to payments.** The refund is with the payments team's systems and normally completes within minutes. If it has been in this state for more than one hour, ask the payments team to check it, quoting the refund number.
2. **Paid.** Give the customer the payment date and the provider reference. Banks take up to five working days to show a card refund and up to two for UPI. Ask the customer to check with their bank using the reference after that time.
3. **Failed.** The money did not leave Meridian. An L2 engineer can retry the payment from the ticket once the cause is understood. If the card is closed, the payments team settles it with the customer's bank. Support never takes new card or bank details.
4. **No refund recorded.** Tell the customer honestly that no refund has been issued. Then check whether one is due under rb-021 or rb-022, and start it properly.

Never tell a customer a refund "has been processed" unless its status is paid.

## Compensation

- A late refund is not itself a reason for a second refund. One goodwill refund per order still applies, and total refunds never exceed the order value.
- If Meridian approved a refund and it sat unpaid for more than three working days through no fault of the bank, apologise and tell a supervisor, who follows it up with the payments team lead.

## Escalation

- L1 engineers escalate to L2 for a failed refund, because only L2 can retry it.
- Escalate to a supervisor when a refund request has been waiting for a supervisor's approval for more than 12 hours, so it does not expire.
- Escalate to the payments team lead if a refund stays in sent to payments for more than one working day.
