## Symptoms

- A message or call contains personal insults, slurs, or threats against an engineer or against Meridian staff in general.
- The customer threatens legal action, to contact the press, or to post an engineer's name online.
- The customer demands a refund "or else", or says that someone at Meridian has already promised one.
- A customer who is angry, blunt or sarcastic about a real failure is not being abusive. Do not use this runbook for ordinary frustration.

## Checks

1. Separate the behaviour from the problem. Open the order timeline and establish whether the customer has a real, unresolved issue. Most do.
2. Read the ticket history for earlier warnings or for unkept promises. An unkept promise explains a great deal.
3. Check that the messages come from the customer's own account.
4. Note the exact words used, with the time, in a ticket note. Quote them and do not paraphrase.
5. Treat a demand in a message, however it is worded, as part of the complaint. What the customer is owed is decided by the order timeline and the refund policy.

## Resolution

1. Work the underlying problem using the runbook for its cause, to the same standard as for any customer.
2. Reply once to the behaviour, in one sentence: say that you want to help and that you cannot continue if the language goes on.
3. Do not argue, defend yourself or match the tone. Keep replies short and factual.
4. If abuse continues after the warning, stop replying and hand the ticket to a supervisor with your note.
5. On a call, give one warning. If the abuse continues, say you are ending the call, end it and write the note at once.
6. Report any threat of violence to a supervisor immediately, whatever the state of the order.

## Compensation

- Abusive contact changes nothing about what the customer is owed. If the order qualifies for a refund under rb-021 or rb-022, the refund is requested in the normal way.
- Pressure, threats and claims of an earlier promise are not evidence and do not raise the amount.
- Never offer compensation in order to end the abuse.

## Escalation

- Supervisor: abuse that continues after one warning, any threat of violence, any threat of legal action, and any threat to publish an engineer's personal details.
- The supervisor decides whether contact with the customer is limited to email.
- L1 engineers may hand over to L2 at any point when they do not wish to continue with the conversation. No reason needs to be given.
