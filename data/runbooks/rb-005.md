## Symptoms

- The customer reports two debits for one order, usually with a bank statement or a screenshot.
- The order itself looks normal and may already be packed or shipped.
- The two debits are for the same amount and a few seconds or minutes apart. This typically follows a payment page that timed out and was retried.
- Less often, the customer has two orders for the same items and believes one is a duplicate charge.

## Checks

1. Search the customer's orders for a second order with the same items placed at about the same time. If there is one, this is a duplicate order, not a duplicate capture: offer to cancel the second order under rb-028.
2. If there is only one order, ask the customer for the date, the amount and the last four digits of the card or the UPI reference for both debits. Do not ask for a full card number.
3. Ask whether the second debit shows as pending or as completed. A pending authorisation normally disappears within a few working days without any action.
4. Check the ticket notes for an earlier report, so the payments team is not asked twice.

## Resolution

1. Send the order number and both payment references to the payments team and ask them to confirm whether two captures exist for the order.
2. If only one capture exists, the second debit is an authorisation that the bank will release. Tell the customer this, and that the bank's timing is usually three to five working days.
3. If two captures exist, the payments team reverses the extra one. This is a reversal of a payment made in error. It is not an order refund and is not recorded on the order as one.
4. Give the customer the reversal reference from the payments team and the expected time for it to reach their account.
5. Keep the ticket open until the payments team confirms the reversal was sent.

## Compensation

- The extra capture is returned in full by the payments team. Support engineers do not request a refund for it, because a refund recorded on the order would reduce the amount that could be refunded later for a real fault.
- A duplicate capture does not delay the order, so it gives no goodwill refund on its own.
- If the order is also delayed, treat the delay separately under rb-021.

## Escalation

- Escalate to L2 if the payments team has not answered within one working day.
- Escalate to a supervisor if the customer reports more than two debits, or if several customers report duplicates on the same day. That suggests a fault with the payment provider.
