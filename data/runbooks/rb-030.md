## Symptoms

- A ticket is ready to be resolved, or has gone quiet and needs a decision.
- A ticket is waiting on the customer and they have not replied.
- A ticket has been open for a long time with no recent note.
- You are taking over a ticket from a colleague and cannot tell from the notes what has happened.

## Checks

Before resolving a ticket, confirm all of the following:

1. The cause has been identified from the order timeline and is written in a note.
2. The fix has happened, not merely been requested: the pickup event exists, the hold is released, the parcel is moving or delivered.
3. The customer has been told the outcome.
4. No refund request on the ticket is waiting for approval, and no refund is approved or sent to payments without yet being paid. A ticket cannot be resolved in either state.
5. A refund that failed has been retried or handed to the payments team. See rb-007.
6. The ticket's priority and queue still match the situation.

## Resolution

1. Write notes that a colleague can act on without asking you: what you found, what you did, and what happens next and when. One note per action.
2. Do not put card numbers, passwords or one-time codes in a note, and do not copy another customer's details into a ticket.
3. Set the ticket to pending customer when you have asked the customer a question and cannot continue without the answer. Say in the note what you asked.
4. If a customer has not replied after three days, send one reminder. After four more days, resolve the ticket with a note saying it can be reopened.
5. Resolve the ticket when the checks above are all met. A resolved ticket closes automatically after seven days without a reply.
6. A customer's reply to a resolved ticket reopens it. Treat a reopened ticket as a repeat contact under rb-026.

## Compensation

- Closing a ticket never involves compensation, and a refund is never offered to get a ticket closed.
- If, while reviewing an old ticket, you find the order qualified for a goodwill refund that nobody offered and the order is still undelivered, follow rb-021 before resolving.
- Do not resolve a ticket to stop a refund request from being looked at.

## Escalation

- Escalate to L2 any open ticket with no note for five days that you cannot resolve yourself.
- An escalated ticket stays with L2 until it is resolved. It is not passed back to L1 to close.
- Tell a supervisor about any ticket resolved while a refund was still in progress, so that it can be reopened.
