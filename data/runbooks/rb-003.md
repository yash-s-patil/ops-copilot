## Symptoms

- The customer asks to change the delivery address, or to deliver to a different person, after placing the order.
- The order is already in `packed` or `shipped`.
- There may be no fault on the order at all. This runbook covers a customer request, not a failure.

## Checks

1. Check the order status. The steps differ for `packed` and `shipped`; an order in `placed` or `paid` can simply be edited.
2. Verify that the person asking is the customer on the order. Ask them to confirm the email address or phone number on the account. Do not read these details out to the caller.
3. Check that the new postcode is serviceable by the carrier assigned to the order.
4. Check whether the new address is in a different region. A change between regions cannot be made on an existing order.

## Resolution

For an order in `packed`:

1. Ask the dispatch desk to hold the parcel. It must be relabelled before the carrier collects it.
2. Update the address on the order and re-trigger address validation.
3. Confirm with the dispatch desk that the label was replaced, then tell the customer the new dispatch date.

For an order in `shipped`:

1. Raise a redirect request with the carrier, quoting the tracking reference. Carriers accept a redirect only before the parcel is out for delivery, and only within the same city.
2. If the carrier refuses, tell the customer the parcel will be delivered to the original address. They may refuse delivery, and the parcel will be returned to origin under rb-015.

For a change to another region, cancel the order under rb-028 if it has not shipped and ask the customer to place a new one.

## Compensation

- A delay that follows from the customer's own change request does not count toward a goodwill refund. The delay clock restarts when the relabelled parcel is handed to the carrier.
- If Meridian or the carrier then causes a further delay, that later delay counts in the normal way under rb-021.
- There is no charge to the customer for a relabel or a redirect.

## Escalation

- Escalate to L2 if identity cannot be confirmed and the caller insists on the change. Changing an address for an unverified caller is how parcels are stolen.
- Escalate to L2 if the dispatch desk reports that the parcel left with the old label.
- A supervisor decides any request to redirect an order worth more than INR 25,000.
