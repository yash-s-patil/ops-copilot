## Symptoms

- The customer has written two or more times on the same ticket without the problem being resolved, or has opened several tickets about one order.
- Messages such as "this is the second time I am writing" or "any update?" appear in the ticket notes.
- Earlier replies were generic, or promised an update that never came.
- The underlying problem is usually simple. What has gone wrong is the handling.

## Checks

1. Find every ticket for the order and read all the notes in order of time.
2. List what the customer has asked and what they were told. Note every promise made to them, kept or not.
3. Identify the cause from the order timeline, not from earlier notes. Earlier notes may be wrong.
4. Check whether the ticket should already have been escalated: a premium ticket older than 48 hours, an order stuck for twice the time in its definition, or a justified refund.
5. Check whether the customer was asked for information they had already given.

## Resolution

1. If there are several tickets for one order, keep the oldest open one, add a note to it listing the others, and resolve the duplicates with a note that points to the ticket being kept.
2. Assign yourself as the owner and tell the customer your name.
3. Reply to the customer's actual question first. If they asked where the parcel is, say where it is.
4. Acknowledge the earlier contacts once, without excuses.
5. Say what you will do and when you will next write, and keep to it. Write at the promised time even if there is no news.
6. Apply the runbook for the underlying cause.

## Compensation

- Repeat contact is not a reason for a refund and does not raise a goodwill refund above the standard offer.
- If the delay behind the contacts has reached the goodwill threshold for the customer's tier, offer the refund without waiting to be asked. See rb-021.
- Do not offer a refund to end the conversation. An offer that policy does not allow will be refused when it is requested, and the customer will have been misled a second time.

## Escalation

- Escalate to L2 when the customer has written three times without a resolution, or when a refund is justified.
- Escalate to a supervisor when earlier replies promised something that policy does not allow, so the supervisor can decide how to put it right.
- For abusive messages, see rb-027.
