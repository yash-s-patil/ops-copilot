summary: "Agree a delivery slot or another recipient. Missed attempts do not count as delay."

## Symptoms

- The order is in `shipped`, and the latest carrier scan is at the delivery hub with the note "attempted" or "customer unavailable".
- The customer may say that nobody came, or that they were at home all day.
- After three failed attempts the carrier returns the parcel to origin. That stage is rb-015.
- The carrier's tracking page shows the number of attempts made.

## Checks

1. Count the delivery attempts on the carrier's tracking page and note the date of each.
2. Confirm the delivery address and the phone number on the order with the customer. Riders call before delivery, and a wrong number causes most failed attempts.
3. Ask the customer whether a missed-delivery card was left and whether they received a call.
4. If the customer insists they were present, ask the carrier for the rider's location record for the attempt. Carriers hold this for seven days.

## Resolution

1. Agree a delivery day with the customer and, where the carrier offers it, a morning or afternoon slot.
2. Offer alternatives if the customer is rarely at home: delivery to a neighbour or security desk they name, a workplace address in the same city, or collection from the carrier's pickup point.
3. Pass the agreed arrangement to the carrier as a delivery instruction, quoting the tracking reference.
4. Remind the customer that the parcel goes back to the warehouse after the third attempt.
5. If the carrier's location record shows the rider did not go to the address, ask the carrier to reset the attempt count and report the false attempt to the dispatch desk lead.

## Compensation

- A delay caused by missed delivery attempts is a delay caused by the customer and does not count toward a goodwill refund. The delay clock restarts when a new delivery arrangement is agreed.
- If the carrier's record shows a false attempt, the delay is the carrier's and counts in the normal way. See rb-021.
- There is no charge to the customer for a second or third attempt.

## Escalation

- Escalate to L2 when the carrier refuses to reset a false attempt, or when the same rider has two false attempts reported.
- Escalate to L2 when a goodwill refund is justified because of a false attempt.
- If the customer cannot be reached and the third attempt is due, set the ticket to pending customer with a note of each contact attempt. Do not cancel the order on the customer's behalf.
